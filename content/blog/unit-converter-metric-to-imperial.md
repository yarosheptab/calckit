For Americans, imperial measures dominate everyday life while metric dominates science, medicine, and global trade.

## The $125 Million NASA Disaster

The most famous unit conversion failure in history: the **Mars Climate Orbiter**, launched in December 1998 and lost on September 23, 1999. The spacecraft was supposed to enter Mars orbit at an altitude of approximately 150–170 km. Instead, it entered at roughly 57 km — far too low — and was destroyed by atmospheric friction.

The cause: one engineering team (Lockheed Martin) delivered thruster force data in **pound-force seconds** (imperial), while NASA's navigation team assumed the data was in **newton-seconds** (metric). The software never flagged the unit mismatch.

Total loss: the spacecraft itself ($327 million), plus the mission cost — approximately $125 million in direct mission expenses. It remains the canonical example of why unit conversions cannot be left to assumption.

## Length Conversions: Metric to Imperial Cheat Sheet

Length is the most commonly converted dimension. The exact conversion factors:

| Metric | Imperial | Conversion Factor |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| 1 kilometer (km) | 0.6214 miles | × 0.6214 |
| 1 meter (m) | 3.2808 feet | × 3.2808 |
| 1 meter (m) | 1.0936 yards | × 1.0936 |
| 1 centimeter (cm) | 0.3937 inches | × 0.3937 |
| 1 millimeter (mm) | 0.03937 inches | × 0.03937 |
| 1 inch | 2.54 cm (exact) | × 2.54 |
| 1 foot | 30.48 cm (exact) | × 30.48 |

The inch-to-centimeter conversion (1 inch = 2.54 cm) is exact — it was legally defined by the international yard and pound agreement of 1959. This is the anchor from which all other length conversions between systems derive.

## Temperature Conversions: Formulas and Benchmarks

Temperature is perhaps the most misunderstood conversion because it is not a simple multiplication — there is an offset involved:

- **°C to °F:** Multiply by 9/5 (1.8), then add 32
  - Formula: °F = (°C × 1.8) + 32
- **°F to °C:** Subtract 32, then multiply by 5/9 (0.5556)
  - Formula: °C = (°F − 32) × 0.5556

Key benchmark temperatures every traveler and cook should know:

| Celsius | Fahrenheit | Context |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| −40°C | −40°F | The only point where the two scales are equal |
| 0°C | 32°F | Water freezes |
| 20°C | 68°F | Comfortable room temperature |
| 37°C | 98.6°F | Normal human body temperature |
| 100°C | 212°F | Water boils (at sea level) |
| 180°C | 356°F | Typical baking temperature |

The −40° equivalence point is genuinely useful: if it is colder than −40 in either scale, you know it is brutally cold in both.

## Weight and Mass Conversions

Weight conversions trip up people in grocery shopping, luggage limits, and fitness:

- 1 kilogram (kg) = **2.2046 pounds (lbs)**
- 1 pound (lb) = **453.592 grams (g)**
- 1 ounce (oz) = **28.3495 grams (g)**
- 1 stone (UK) = **14 pounds** = 6.350 kg

Common real-world applications:

- A 23 kg airline baggage limit = 50.7 lbs
- A 100 lb person weighs 45.4 kg
- A 16 oz (1 lb) steak = 453.6 g

## Digital Storage Units: The Binary vs. Decimal Problem

Data units involve a different kind of conversion confusion — binary (powers of 2) vs. decimal (powers of 10):

| Unit | Binary Definition | Decimal (SI) Definition |
|------|------------------|------------------------|
| 1 KB (kilobyte) | 1,024 bytes | 1,000 bytes |
| 1 MB (megabyte) | 1,048,576 bytes | 1,000,000 bytes |
| 1 GB (gigabyte) | 1,073,741,824 bytes | 1,000,000,000 bytes |
| 1 TB (terabyte) | 1,099,511,627,776 bytes | 1,000,000,000,000 bytes |

Storage device manufacturers use **decimal** definitions (1 GB = 1,000,000,000 bytes) because the numbers appear larger. Operating systems historically used **binary** definitions. This is why a "128 GB" SSD shows as approximately **119 GB** in Windows or macOS — the device is using decimal GB, the OS is reporting in binary GB.

The difference grows with scale: a "1 TB" drive appears as approximately **909 GB** in traditional binary reporting.

## Mental Shortcuts for Quick Conversions

When you don't have a unit converter handy, these approximations are accurate enough for most everyday purposes:

- **km to miles:** Multiply by 0.6 (exact: 0.6214). A 10 km run ≈ 6.2 miles.
- **miles to km:** Multiply by 1.6 (exact: 1.609). A 26.2-mile marathon ≈ 42 km.
- **kg to lbs:** Multiply by 2.2. A 70 kg person ≈ 154 lbs.
- **°C to °F:** Double the Celsius, add 30 (rough). 20°C → 40 + 30 = 70°F (actual: 68°F).
- **liters to gallons (US):** Multiply by 0.264. A 2-liter bottle ≈ 0.53 gallons.
