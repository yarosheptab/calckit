## Why Salary-to-Hourly Conversion Matters More Than You Think

A **salary to hourly calculator** is not just a math shortcut — it's a decision-making tool. When you know your hourly equivalent, you can compare job offers fairly, decide whether overtime is worth it, price consulting work accurately, and evaluate whether a task is worth your time.

Most people only see the annual number on an offer letter. Converting it to an hourly rate reveals what your employer is actually paying per unit of your time — and sometimes that number is surprising.

## The Math: How to Convert Annual Salary to Hourly

The standard work year in the US is **2,080 hours**: 52 weeks × 40 hours per week. Divide your annual salary by 2,080 to get your gross hourly rate.

**Hourly = Annual Salary ÷ 2,080**

For $52,000/year: $52,000 ÷ 2,080 = **$25.00/hour**

That's the baseline formula. Simple, but it changes depending on actual hours worked.

## The Work Year Isn't Always 2,080 Hours

The 2,080-hour assumption is a starting point, not a universal truth:

- **2,080 hours**: standard (no holidays, no vacation)
- **2,000 hours**: 52 weeks × 40 hours minus ~10 federal holidays
- **1,960 hours**: 2,000 hours minus 2 weeks of paid vacation
- **1,840 hours**: 2,000 hours minus 2 weeks vacation + 2 weeks sick leave

Why does this matter? If you're comparing a salary that includes 3 weeks of paid vacation vs. a contract rate with no paid time off, you need to normalize for actual working hours. A $75,000 salaried role with 15 vacation days works out to ~$39.47/hour on actual days worked — notably higher than the raw $36.06 calculation.

## Gross vs Net: What You Actually Take Home

Gross hourly rate is what you earn before taxes. Net hourly rate — what hits your bank account — is significantly lower.

For a $75,000 annual salary (single filer, standard deduction):
- **Gross hourly**: $36.06
- **Federal income tax**: ~$8,114/year (~$3.90/hour)
- **FICA (Social Security + Medicare)**: ~$5,738/year (~$2.76/hour)
- **State income tax** (varies, estimate 4%): ~$3,000/year (~$1.44/hour)
- **Net hourly rate (estimated)**: ~**$26–28/hour**

So $36.06 gross becomes roughly $27 net — a 25% reduction. This matters when comparing a salary offer to a contract rate: the contract rate needs to be higher to deliver equivalent take-home pay. Model the breakdown with the [tax calculator](/tax-calculator).

## Overtime Rules: Who Gets Paid More for Extra Hours

Under the **Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA)**, hourly (non-exempt) workers must be paid at least 1.5× their regular rate for hours over 40 per week. That's federal law — some states have stricter rules requiring daily overtime.

For a worker earning $25/hour:
- Regular time: $25.00/hour
- Overtime: $37.50/hour

Salaried "exempt" employees (most white-collar workers earning over ~$35,568/year) generally do not receive overtime — their salary covers all hours worked. This is a key factor when comparing salaried vs hourly roles, especially in jobs with frequent extra hours.

## Setting Freelance and Consulting Rates

The **hourly to annual salary** conversion works in reverse for freelancers. If you want to earn $80,000 as a freelancer, your hourly rate should not simply be $80,000 ÷ 2,080 = $38.46.

Why? Because freelancers pay:
- **Self-employment tax**: 15.3% (vs employee's 7.65%)
- **Health insurance** out of pocket: $400–$800+/month
- **No paid vacation, sick leave, or retirement match**
- **Business expenses, software, equipment**

A practical freelance rate formula: **multiply your target income by 1.3–1.5× to cover these costs**, then divide by your billable hours (typically 1,000–1,400/year once you account for non-billable admin, marketing, and downtime).

For $80,000 target income, needing $104,000–$120,000 in gross revenue, billing 1,200 hours: **$87–$100/hour** is a realistic freelance rate.

## Comparing Total Compensation, Not Just Base Salary

When evaluating job offers, base salary is only part of the **pay period calculator** picture. Total compensation includes:

- **Base salary** (the hourly-equivalent number)
- **Annual bonus** (10–20% is common in tech/finance)
- **Equity/stock** (RSUs, options — often vest over 4 years)
- **401(k) match** (often 3–6% of salary, free money)
- **Health insurance** (employer-provided plans can be worth $10,000–$20,000/year)
- **Paid time off** value (15 vacation days at $36/hour = $4,320)

A $90,000 offer with a 15% bonus, 5% 401k match, and generous benefits may be worth more than a $100,000 offer with no benefits. Always build the full compensation picture before accepting or declining.

## Key Takeaways

- Annual salary ÷ 2,080 = gross hourly rate (standard US work year)
- $75,000/year = $36.06/hour gross, but only ~$26–28/hour net after taxes
- Actual billable hours vary: 2,000 with holidays, 1,960 with 2 weeks vacation
- Non-exempt hourly workers earn 1.5× for overtime hours; salaried exempt workers generally do not
- Freelancers need 1.3–1.5× their income target to cover self-employment tax and benefits
- Always compare total compensation — benefits, bonus, and equity can be worth 20–40% of base salary
